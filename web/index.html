How the model was built

No single method wins at a single-city, suburb-level, 5-year horizon, so this is a four-layer hybrid. Each layer is placed where it is strongest, and the forecast is reproducible: the Python in /model regenerates the exact baseline numbers and self-verifies.

Layer 1

City VECM spine

An error-correction model ties Perth's price to its fundamentals (income, rents, construction costs, the real cash rate) via a long-run equilibrium, and judges how over- or under-valued it is today. It calibrates the scenario anchor paths.

Layer 2

Scenario Monte Carlo

Three anchor paths (bear / base / bull) to Mid-29, weighted 30 / 50 / 20, produce the probability-weighted expected path and the spread band you see in the fan chart. Expected = round-half-up to the nearest $1,000 of the weighted average.

Layer 3

Suburb panel

A gradient-boosted-style panel scores relative suburb performance from school zone, distance to Como, land, KDR economics, growth and family fit. In this repo it is a transparent weighted composite (scoring.py) that mirrors the tool exactly.

Layer 4

Hedonic adjustment

Applied per listing: what an extra 100sqm of land, a 4th bedroom or knock-down-rebuild optionality adds. This is where the 500sqm filter and the KDR criterion bite, confirmed property by property via Landgate.

The variables: standard inputs, enriched with leading signals

A generic model stops at rates, migration and approvals. This one adds family-buyer-specific leading indicators most analysts miss, with a strict signal discipline: A leads price and is prioritised, B is useful but slower, C is corroboration only.

Appendix

Scenario assumptions

Base (50%) RBA holds 4.35% into 2027 then eases; iron ore ~US$95/t in 2026 easing to ~US$85-90 in 2027; undersupply floors prices; the 1 Jul 2027 negative-gearing / CGT change trims investor demand by ~0.6-1.0ppt. Flat-to-modest growth, re-acceleration from 2028.

Bear (30%) renewed RBA hiking plus iron ore under US$85/t and a faster Simandou ramp; sharper investor pull-back. A trough around -7% late-27 to mid-28; undersupply prevents a 2014-style crash.

Bull (20%) RBA cuts in 2027, migration stays above 2% with completions still short, construction-cost relief. Prices re-accelerate and family school-zone suburbs lead.

Key numbers (baseline 30 / 50 / 20)

  • Expected Mid-29: $1.17M, expected 3-year CAGR ~5.4%
  • Bear floor (worst case): $930k, roughly -7%
  • Bull ceiling (best case) Mid-29: $1.40M
  • Buy window: the 2027 soft patch (Mid-27 to End-27)

Horizon

3 years primary, 5-year scenario tail. Forecast error compounds and policy uncertainty beyond 3 years is large; the 2027 soft patch and the bear trough both sit inside three years, so the spine runs to Mid-29 with the tail as commentary.

Reproducibility

Run python3 model/scenario_model.py to regenerate the forecast (it self-verifies against data/baseline.json and prints PASS) and python3 model/scoring.py for the suburb ranking. Standard library only. The Reset button restores exactly this baseline.

The ten criteria

The brief, in full. Three are hard filters: a suburb that can't deliver them is marked down hard. The rest are weighted and combined into the fit score. The buyer's low holding cost isn't a filter; it's why patience is affordable.

How this model works. A four-layer hybrid: a city-level mean-reversion spine, a probability-weighted scenario engine (the fan chart), a suburb-level relative-performance layer (the heat map), and a property-level hedonic adjustment for land and knock-down-rebuild potential. It is reproducible; the Python in /model regenerates these exact baseline numbers. Full methodology is in the project's skills folder.
